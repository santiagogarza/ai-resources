# Product Psychology Masterclass — Course Modules

Source: Growth.Design Product Psychology Masterclass (https://growth.design/members/courses/product-psychology)

## Course Overview

The Product Psychology Masterclass teaches how to use psychology to build products people love. It covers user behavior understanding, systematic experience improvement, journey design, stakeholder communication, and ethical design.

---

## Module 1: Understand User Behaviors

**Goal**: Learn how to use the psychology of stories to build user empathy, how to motivate people to act, and what impacts people's ability to interact with your product.

### Lessons
1. **Stories** — Using the psychology of stories to build user empathy
2. **Behavior Mapping** — How to map motivation, ability, and prompts
3. **Psych Framework** — A framework for analyzing psychological patterns in your product

### Key Frameworks

#### The 6P Story Framework
A tool for understanding how your product fits in the real-life story of your customer:
- **Person**: Who is your user? What's their context?
- **Problem**: What pain or need do they have?
- **Promise**: What does your product promise to solve?
- **Process**: How does the user interact with your product?
- **Proof**: What evidence shows the product delivers?
- **Payoff**: What's the outcome for the user?

#### Behavior Mapping (Fogg Behavior Model)
For any desired user action, three elements must converge simultaneously:
- **Motivation**: The user must want to do it (hopes, pains, social drivers)
- **Ability**: The user must be able to do it (time, money, effort, mental load, practice)
- **Prompt**: The user must be nudged at the right moment

If behavior isn't happening, diagnose which element is missing or insufficient.

#### General Empathy Questions (GEQs)
Three broad questions to understand your users deeply. Get at least 5 customer answers for each. Use surveys or direct conversations.

#### Specific Empathy Questions (SEQs)
Three targeted questions to identify Behavior Blockers and Enablers for both successful and dropout customers.

## Module 2: Find The Gaps — The B.I.A.S. Framework

**Goal**: Learn the 4 mental steps people go through when interacting with any product and how they think. Know exactly how to use the most impactful psychology principles to improve your product.

### Lessons
1. **B.I.A.S. Framework** (overview)
2. **B.I.A.S. Block** — How information gets filtered
3. **B.I.A.S. Interpret** — How users make sense of what they see
4. **B.I.A.S. Act** — How users take action (or don't)
5. **B.I.A.S. Store** — How users remember the experience
6. **B.I.A.S. Recap** — Putting it all together

### The B.I.A.S. Framework in Detail

#### B — Block (Information Filtering)
Ask: "Does this pass the user's brain filters?"
- Is it noticeable? (Not buried, not ad-like, not in a blind spot)
- Is it low-effort looking? (Not intimidating)
- Is it unexpected/interesting? (Pattern interrupts)
- Is it well-timed? (Aligned with current behavior/context)

#### I — Interpret (Meaning Making)
Ask: "What does the user understand from this?"
- Is cognitive load minimized?
- Does it build on familiar patterns?
- Are there good comparison anchors?
- Are benefits clearly tied to user's hopes?
- Are key actions discoverable?
- Is fear of loss addressed constructively?

#### A — Act (Decision & Action)
Ask: "How easily can the user take action?"
- How many decisions per page?
- Can options be reduced?
- Are there smart defaults?
- Is it broken into smaller steps?
- Are features revealed gradually?

#### S — Store (Memory & Satisfaction)
Ask: "Will the user remember this positively?"
- Are basic expectations met?
- Is there clear feedback on every action?
- Does the user feel reassured?
- Are there signs you care about their outcome?
- Are there delight opportunities?
- Is the overall relationship positive?

## Module 3: Create Delightful Journeys

**Goal**: Discover how to find your customer's most important moments (highest ROI) and proven techniques to turn an average experience into a delightful one.

### Lessons
1. **Mapping Journeys** — How to map the emotional journey of your users
2. **Improving Journeys** — Techniques for elevating the experience

### Key Concepts

#### Journey Components
- **Jumps**: Moments of high positive emotion — find and amplify them
- **Drops**: Moments of negative emotion — find and fix/mitigate them
- **Peaks**: The highest emotional point in the journey
- **Pits**: The lowest emotional point in the journey
- **Transitions**: Moments where the experience shifts (onboarding to usage, free to paid, etc.)

#### Peak-End Rule
Users judge the entire experience based on two moments: the peak (most intense) and the end. Design accordingly:
- Make sure the peak is intentionally great
- Always end on a high note
- A mediocre experience with a great ending feels better than a good experience with a bad ending

#### Labor Illusion
Users value things more when they see the work behind them. Waiting periods can be transformed into value by showing effort ("Searching 1,247 options for you...").

## Module 4: Communicate and Justify Product Decisions

**Goal**: Learn the best way to understand and positively influence stakeholders' decisions and practical tactics for your next meeting.

### Lessons
1. **Communicating Decisions** — Frameworks for presenting product decisions

### Key Techniques

#### Before a Product Review Meeting
1. Clarify the goal of the review
2. Define what you want feedback on (create guardrails)
3. Articulate what problem this solution solves
4. Prepare the user story that illustrates the problem
5. Explain why this solution is better than alternatives

#### Communication Framework
- Lead with the business goal
- Tell a compelling user story to make the problem tangible
- Use psychological principle vocabulary to add credibility and precision
- Frame alternatives and explain your reasoning for the chosen path

## Module 5: Make Ethical Decisions as a Team

**Goal**: Learn how to evaluate your product using ethical tests and fundamental principles for crafting more humane experiences.

### Lessons
1. **Ethics** — Ethical frameworks for product decisions

### Ethical Tests

#### The Regret Test
When something feels questionable:
- "If a user was in the room with you while you discuss this feature, would you say the same things?"
- "If users knew what your team knows, would they take the same action?"

#### The Black Mirror Test
Even if you believe what you're doing is good for users:
- "What would using your product 'too much' look like?"
- "Who or what disappears if your feature becomes very successful?"
- List all potential negative side effects
- Brainstorm how to prevent those scenarios

#### Ethical Considerations Checklist
- **Scarcity**: Are you using real scarcity or manufactured urgency?
- **Defaults**: Are defaults set to the user's advantage?
- **Completion**: Do you offer real exit points?
- **Control**: Do users control when and what they receive?

#### Humane Principles
- Does the product save users time?
- Does it respect users' attention?
- Does it reflect human values?
- Does it push humanity forward?

#### The "In Real Life" Test
Imagine your screens and interactions as real people — what would they look like, say, and act like? Is it someone you'd want to be friends with?

## Cross-Module Connections

The modules build on each other in a deliberate sequence:
1. **Understand** (Module 1) → Know your user deeply through empathy and behavior mapping
2. **Analyze** (Module 2) → Use B.I.A.S. to find gaps in the current experience
3. **Design** (Module 3) → Create journeys that hit the right emotional notes
4. **Communicate** (Module 4) → Get buy-in from stakeholders with psychological vocabulary
5. **Validate** (Module 5) → Ensure your solutions pass ethical tests
6. **Certify** (Module 6) → Demonstrate mastery
